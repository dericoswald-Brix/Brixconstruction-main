Does RERA apply if you're building your own home?

RERA protects apartment buyers from developers. If you're building your own house on your own plot, the rules that actually apply to you are different, and nobody explains that part.

B
Brix Construction Civil engineers & site execution · Bangalore

Almost everything written about RERA in Bangalore is written for someone buying a flat from a developer. If you're building your own house on land you already own, most of it doesn't actually apply to you, and that gap in the coverage is exactly where the confusion starts. Here's what's true, what isn't, and what actually protects you instead.

Myth 01
"RERA protects me no matter how I build my home."
RERA exists to regulate developers who market and sell real estate projects to buyers. It requires registration for projects on land over 500 square metres, or with more than 8 units, and forces the developer to put 70% of collected funds into an escrow account.
What's actually true
If you already own your plot and are hiring a contractor to build one house on it, you're not buying a unit from a developer. That relationship, and the risks in it, sit outside what RERA was built to regulate.
Myth 02
"My contractor needs to be RERA registered."
RERA registration is a promoter/developer obligation, tied to marketing and selling a project to buyers, not to the act of construction itself.
What's actually true
A construction company executing a building contract for you, on your land, is providing a service under a construction agreement, not selling you real estate. RERA registration simply doesn't apply to that role.
Myth 03
"I bought my plot in a layout, so RERA has nothing to do with me."
This is where it gets genuinely relevant. If the plotted layout you bought into was itself developed and sold by a builder above the threshold, that layout should have been RERA-registered before plots were sold.
What's actually true
Worth checking the layout's RERA number on rera.karnataka.gov.in, even though the house itself is being built by your own contractor. It tells you whether the land, roads, and common infrastructure promised to you are legally accounted for.
Myth 04
"If RERA doesn't apply, I have no real protection."
This is the one that actually matters. RERA not applying doesn't mean you're unprotected, it means your protection comes from a different document entirely.
What's actually true
A properly written construction contract, with a detailed BOQ, payments tied to verified progress rather than upfront lump sums, and clear structural warranty terms, does for an owner-builder what RERA does for a flat buyer.

Karnataka RERA is getting more active, for what it's worth

Karnataka's RERA authority has been enforcing more visibly through 2026, including digital complaint tools and rulings against developers. In January 2026, K-RERA ordered a Bengaluru developer to refund over ₹52 lakh to a homebuyer over unfair GST billing. That's a real, useful protection, if you're the kind of buyer RERA was built for. It just isn't the mechanism protecting you if you're commissioning your own build.

What to actually rely on instead

  • A detailed, written BOQ specifying material grades, brands, and quantities, not a vague lump-sum quote.
  • Payment milestones tied to verified site progress, never a large upfront amount before work has started.
  • Clear structural warranty terms in the contract itself, in writing, not a verbal assurance.
  • Your own approvals in order: sanctioned building plan, khata, and eventual completion certificate through BBMP.

These are the documents that actually carry weight if something goes wrong on an owner-built home, in a way that citing RERA to your contractor simply won't.

Frequently asked questions

Does RERA apply if I'm building a house on my own plot?

Usually no. RERA registration applies to developers above a certain project size, typically land over 500 square metres or more than 8 units. A single house built by an individual owner on their own plot generally falls outside that.

Does my contractor need to be RERA registered?

No. RERA registration is a developer/promoter obligation tied to selling a project, not a requirement for a construction agency executing a building contract.

What actually protects me if RERA doesn't apply?

A properly structured construction contract with a detailed BOQ, progress-based payments, clear structural warranty terms, and compliance with BBMP approvals like the sanctioned plan and khata.
