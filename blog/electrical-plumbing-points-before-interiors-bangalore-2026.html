The points you'll regret not planning before interiors begin

August 2026 10 min read By Wood & Co. Interiors

No plug beside the bed. A chimney point at the wrong height. A washing machine tap on the opposite wall from its socket. Here's the room-by-room checklist to lock in before conduits get chased into your walls.

If you've ever lived in a home and wondered why there's no switch within reach of the bed, or why the router has to sit on the floor because there's no point near the TV unit, you've felt the cost of this exact gap. Most electrical and plumbing points get placed by a civil contractor working off standard practice, not your actual furniture layout, because at that stage nobody's thought about where the sofa or the bed will go yet.

If you're building your own home, this is entirely avoidable, but only if it's decided at the right moment. Once plastering starts, every point is locked in. Anything missed after that means cutting into a finished wall to fix it.

Why this can't wait until interiors start

Electrical conduits are chased into walls or cast into the slab, and plumbing lines are laid, before plastering happens. That's a civil-stage decision, not an interior-stage one. By the time an interior team walks in to install a modular kitchen or wardrobes, the wall is already finished. If a point is missing at that point, the only options are surface-mounted conduit, which looks exactly like what it is, or cutting into the plastered and painted wall to add it properly.

This is also exactly the gap that causes the most regret in Indian homes: the civil contractor and the interior designer are usually two separate people who never actually spoke to each other during the point-marking stage.

How to actually get this right

The fix isn't complicated, it just requires the two sides to talk to each other before the point of no return.

  • Get your interior furniture layout finalized, even roughly, before your electrician starts marking points.
  • Walk the site in person with both your civil contractor and interior designer present, and physically mark each point on the wall together.
  • Cross-check plumbing and electrical points for the same appliance, like a washing machine, are on the same wall.
  • Confirm appliance specifications (chimney model, geyser capacity) before fixing point heights, not after.
  • Only sign off on point marking once you've mentally walked through using each room.

This is the one coordination step that's genuinely hard to pull off when your civil contractor and interior team are two unrelated companies working on their own timelines. It's also exactly why we handle both under one roof, there's no handoff for these details to fall through.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't electrical and plumbing points be added after interior work starts?

Conduits and pipes are chased into walls or cast into the slab before plastering. Adding a point afterward means cutting into a finished, painted wall and patching it, more expensive and never quite as clean as the original.

Who decides where electrical points go, the civil contractor or the interior designer?

Ideally both, together. A contractor places points based on standard practice, not your furniture layout. Without coordination, points end up not matching where things actually go.

When exactly should I finalize these points?

Before plastering begins. Walk the site with your furniture layout in hand and mark every point with your electrician and plumber before conduits are chased in.

General guidance for planning purposes. Exact point placement should always be confirmed on-site against your specific furniture and appliance layout.
